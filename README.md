# cosmos-canvas

Cosmos world models as [carla_canvas](https://github.com/hakuturu583/carla_canvas) renderers: the ego's sensors in CARLA are generated by a world model, closed loop, behind the CARLA API. A scenario (e.g. [autoware_carla_scenario](https://github.com/autowarefoundation/autoware_carla_scenario)'s `canvas.config`) picks one by its carla_canvas configuration:

| backend | model | sensors | conditioning, drawn from the live scene for every chunk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cosmos_canvas.omnidreams:OmniDreamsRenderer` | OmniDreams (Cosmos 2.5, distilled) through [FlashDreams](https://github.com/NVIDIA/flashdreams), in-process | one camera | an HD-map raster (lane lines, road borders, crosswalks, traffic lights, the road users' boxes) |
| `cosmos_canvas.sim_auto:Cosmos3Renderer` | [Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto) through [cosmos-framework](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-framework) | the Hyperion 8 cameras and a Pandar128 LiDAR | WSM v4 controls and LiDAR HD-map range maps |

Each backend runs in the environment its model needs (its own `renderer.command`). Both export the map from the OpenDRIVE carla_canvas sends, with [roadgen](https://pypi.org/project/roadgen/), and extrapolate the ego's and the actors' poses over the chunk being generated (`cosmos_canvas/trajectory.py`).

The package was `cosmos3-canvas`: `renderer.backend: cosmos3_canvas:Cosmos3Renderer` still works.

## OmniDreams (`cosmos_canvas.omnidreams`)

```yaml
renderer:
  backend: cosmos_canvas.omnidreams:OmniDreamsRenderer
  startup_timeout: 900
  timeout: 120
  options:
    config: omnidreams                        # FlashDreams' OmniDreams configuration: NVIDIA's public checkpoint
    fps: 30                                   # its frame rate
    raster: ludus                             # its HD-map raster, drawn by FlashDreams' ludus
    camera: /path/to/camera_front_wide_120fov.json   # the camera it generates (scripts/ftheta_camera.py)
    first_image: /path/to/first_frame.png
    caption: "A forward-facing dashcam view of an urban drive in clear daytime, on a dry road."
    map_extras: /path/to/Town10HD_Opt.extras.json
sensors:
  - {type: sensor.camera.rgb, role_name: front}
```

FlashDreams' `omnidreams` configuration downloads NVIDIA's checkpoint from Hugging Face ([nvidia/omni-dreams-models](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/omni-dreams-models), NVIDIA Open Model License). Its camera is NVIDIA's `camera_front_wide_120fov`, an f-theta lens: `scripts/ftheta_camera.py` writes the `camera` file from the rig of one of NVIDIA's scenes ([nvidia/omni-dreams-scenes](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/omni-dreams-scenes)), at the generated size.

A fine-tuned checkpoint takes its own configuration (`config`, its weights through `checkpoint` and `checkpoint_env`), frame rate (`fps`), camera and, if it was trained on another HD-map raster, that raster: `raster: package.module:Class` names a raster from outside this package (`cosmos_canvas/omnidreams/raster.py` says what it provides). See [examples/omnidreams.yaml](examples/omnidreams.yaml) for every option.

### How it works

The model generates one camera at its checkpoint's frame rate (`fps`: 30 Hz for NVIDIA's public one), 5 frames in its first chunk and 8 in every later one, each chunk continuing the previous ones (FlashDreams' `initialize_cache` / `generate` / `finalize`, driven directly). Every chunk's input is an HD-map raster per frame, drawn here from the live scene, so the road users a scenario moves (and that react to the ego) are in every chunk. FlashDreams' own OmniDreams server takes the moving objects once per session instead.

| module | what it does |
|---|---|
| `ludus.py` | `raster: ludus`, for NVIDIA's public checkpoint: the raster FlashDreams' ludus draws, with the settings NVIDIA's apps in FlashDreams (`interactive_drive`, `omnidreams_game_engine`) feed the checkpoint with. The map is roadgen's ClipGT export of the OpenDRIVE, read by ludus' ClipGT loader once lane-line styles are in the ClipGT vocabulary and lane lines and road boundaries are cut inside intersections; traffic lights come from the map's extras (OpenDRIVE places them at the ground), grey; the road users are boxes coloured by class, per chunk. |
| `raster.py` | How a raster is named (`ludus`, or `module:Class` from outside the package) and what it is handed per chunk: the frames' times, the ego's `base_link`, the generated camera's poses and the road users' boxes. |
| `mapping.py` | Map helpers the rasters share: roadgen's intersection areas and the stretches of a line outside them, and the map's extras: signal heads and parked vehicles, which carla_canvas does not send (`scripts/export_map_extras.py`, once per map). |
| `renderer.py` | The carla_canvas renderer. It tracks the ego and the actors' boxes, and extrapolates them over the chunk (`trajectory.py`). It generates a chunk when a camera tick needs one of its frames, and turns the frame nearest the tick into the CARLA camera's view. |
| `rig.py` | The generated camera on the ego's `base_link` (rear axle), read from a JSON calibration: a pinhole, or an f-theta lens as NVIDIA's. A CARLA camera on the ego gets the generated frame turned, rotation only, from where the frame was drawn to where the camera is: any pinhole within the generated view, at the same point or a few centimetres from it. With `reproject: false` the CARLA camera stands in for the generated one and gets the frame's centred band at its aspect, resized: for a planner trained on that very camera, this reproduces its training crop, which a symmetric CARLA pinhole cannot when the camera's principal point is off centre. |
| `stream.py` | FlashDreams' OmniDreams pipeline, one chunk at a time. |

The first chunk continues an image of the scene's start (`first_image`, the generated size): a real frame, or one made by a text-to-video model from the same scene.

### The first image, from NVIDIA's Transfer-Auto

For NVIDIA's checkpoint, [Cosmos3-Nano-Transfer-Auto](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos3-Nano-Transfer-Auto) makes the first image from the scene alone: text-to-video from a WSM v4 control and a caption, of which the first frame is kept (`cosmos_canvas/omnidreams/first_image.py`):

1. Capture the control with the renderer `cosmos_canvas.omnidreams.first_image:WsmCapture`, in a scenario or through `scripts/replay_tracks.py` (see [examples/omnidreams_first_image.yaml](examples/omnidreams_first_image.yaml)). It draws the scene's first 93 frames (30 Hz) as `camera_front_wide_120fov`'s WSM v4 control, with the Sim-Auto backend's per-frame WSM renderer, from the OmniDreams camera's mount on the ego, and writes `wsm.mp4`, the caption, `request.jsonl` and `lens.json`. It runs where the Sim-Auto backend runs (cosmos-framework's `cosmos_auto`, a GPU for its renderer).
2. Generate with cosmos-framework:

   ```bash
   torchrun --nproc-per-node=1 -m cosmos_framework.scripts.inference -i <out_dir>/request.jsonl -o <out_dir>/gen \
     --checkpoint-path <Cosmos3-Nano-Transfer-Auto snapshot> --resolution 720 --num-steps 35 \
     --per-view-negative-prompt "$(cat <snapshot>/assets/sample_data/daytime_scene/captions/negative_prompt.txt)" \
     --no-guardrails
   ```

3. Turn its first frame into the OmniDreams camera's view (Transfer-Auto generates the Hyperion camera's lens at 1280x720):

   ```bash
   python -m cosmos_canvas.omnidreams.first_image pick <out_dir>/gen/<name>/<video>.mp4 <out_dir>/lens.json \
     camera_front_wide_120fov.json first_image.png
   ```

### Install

The renderer needs:

- FlashDreams with an OmniDreams configuration for the weights (its container image is the simplest environment); `raster: ludus` uses its ludus renderer;
- `carla-canvas`, `roadgen` and Pillow on top. In FlashDreams' image, which has numpy, scipy, OpenCV and protobuf 7.35, a target directory on `PYTHONPATH` is enough:

```bash
uv pip install --target deps --python-version 3.12 --no-deps carla-canvas==0.2.1 roadgen==0.37.1 'grpcio>=1.84'
```

### Check without CARLA

`scripts/replay_tracks.py` replays a recorded run (a tracks document: per actor, poses in CARLA's frame) through the renderer interface tick by tick, with a CARLA camera on the ego, as the carla_canvas service would call it. It writes the camera's images and the time each tick took.

### Limits

- One camera is generated: CARLA cameras on the ego look through it (black outside its field of view), or with `reproject: false` get it as it is.
- The first chunk needs an image of the scene's start; Transfer-Auto's needs the scene's next 3 s (a recorded run, or the scenario's first seconds captured).
- Traffic-light states are not sent by carla_canvas: lights are drawn without a state (grey in ludus, as NVIDIA's apps draw them).
- The chunk's later frames are drawn at extrapolated poses; a frame is shown rotated to where the camera turned out to be (translation is not corrected).

## Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto (`cosmos_canvas.sim_auto`)

[Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto) as a [carla_canvas](https://github.com/hakuturu583/carla_canvas) renderer: the ego's RGB cameras and ray-cast LiDAR in CARLA are generated by the world model, closed loop, behind the CARLA API.

```yaml
renderer:
  backend: cosmos_canvas.sim_auto:Cosmos3Renderer
  startup_timeout: 900
  timeout: 600
  options:
    checkpoint: /path/to/Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Auto
    cameras: [camera_front_wide_120fov]
    caption: "A daytime drive through a dry, sunlit city street with clear lane markings."
    horizon_seconds: 40
sensors:
  - sensor.lidar.ray_cast
  - sensor.camera.rgb
```

See [examples/cosmos3.yaml](examples/cosmos3.yaml) for a complete file, with the renderer started from its own environment.

### How it works

The model generates the Hyperion 8 cameras and the Pandar128 LiDAR together, one 0.4 s chunk at a time:

- 12 camera frames at 30 fps;
- 4 sweeps at 10 Hz;
- the first chunk is frame 0 and sweep 0.

| module | what it does |
|---|---|
| `stream.py` | Drives the framework's rolling sampler (`iter_rolling_transfer_ar`) one chunk at a time. The model, the plans, the text and the caption schedule are built by framework code from a two-chunk bootstrap request. The control and target latents are then latent buffers covering the whole rollout. Each chunk's controls are encoded with the VAEs' causal streaming encoders, and its outputs decoded with their streaming decoders. |
| `controls.py` | Draws the controls with the cookbook's renderers (`cosmos_auto`), one frame or sweep at a time: WSM v4 control frames, and LiDAR HD-map range maps. The map is the CARLA map's OpenDRIVE, exported to RDS-HQ with [roadgen](https://pypi.org/project/roadgen/). The poses and boxes come from the live scene. The renderers' steps that read the whole clip are replaced by ones that read only the past (the module's docstring lists them). |
| `renderer.py` | The carla_canvas renderer. It tracks the ego's sensors and the actors, and extrapolates their poses for the rest of a chunk (`trajectory.py`, from lidarverse_canvas). It generates a chunk when a tick needs one. |
| `sensors.py` | Turns the outputs into measurements. A CARLA camera gets its Hyperion camera's generated F-theta (fisheye) image as it is. A LiDAR ray reads the range-map cell it points at. |

Checked against the framework's batch inference on the same controls:

- The text tokens and plans are identical.
- The control latents differ by 0.3–0.8 % (camera, BF16 encode chunking) and 0 (LiDAR).
- The first frame agrees to 38 dB PSNR (`scripts/check_stream.py`, `scripts/diag_stream.py`).

### Install

The renderer needs:

- a [cosmos-framework](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-framework) checkout and its environment;
- the cookbook's control renderers (`.agents/skills/cookbooks/cosmos3/simulation/auto/wsm`, with Git LFS);
- `carla-canvas` and `roadgen`.

On GPUs FlashAttention-4 has no kernels for (sm120: RTX PRO 6000, RTX 50 series), the model needs [NVIDIA/cosmos-framework#302](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-framework/pull/302). Until it is merged, use the branch `fix/sm120-gapped-replay` of [hakuturu583/cosmos-framework](https://github.com/hakuturu583/cosmos-framework/tree/fix/sm120-gapped-replay).

carla-canvas needs protobuf ≥ 7.35. In the framework's environment that breaks wandb < 0.31, which the model imports. One way to set it up is a venv on top of the framework's:

```bash
uv venv -p cosmos-framework/.venv/bin/python canvas-venv
SP=canvas-venv/lib/python3.13/site-packages
echo "import site; site.addsitedir('$PWD/cosmos-framework/.venv/lib/python3.13/site-packages')" > $SP/zz_cosmos_framework.pth
printf '%s\n' $PWD/cosmos-framework/.agents/skills/cookbooks/cosmos3/simulation/auto/wsm $PWD/cosmos-canvas > $SP/cosmos_canvas_src.pth
uv pip install -p canvas-venv/bin/python carla-canvas==0.2.0 roadgen==0.37.1 'moderngl>=5.10' 'python-pycg>=0.7' mpire minio 'shapely>=2.1'
uv pip install -p canvas-venv/bin/python --no-deps 'wandb>=0.31'
# then remove from canvas-venv every package the framework's venv already has, except grpcio and protobuf
```

### Speed

On an RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q, one camera at 480p and the LiDAR, a 0.4 s chunk takes about 8.1 s, about 20x slower than real time:

| step | seconds |
|---|---|
| drawing the controls | 0.3 |
| encoding them | 0.47 |
| generating | 6.5 (4 denoising steps and the cache refresh) |
| decoding | 0.8 |

A 10 s scenario takes about 4 minutes. The generation is bound by the transformer's matrix multiplies. About 70 % of a chunk's tokens are the LiDAR's.

### Cameras

The cameras are the model's: the Hyperion 8 cameras, each through its own F-theta lens (the cookbook's intrinsics), generated at the run's `resolution` (832x480 at `480`). A CARLA camera standing for one gets that fisheye image as it is, so it must be exactly that camera:

- its size, the generated frames' (832x480 at `480`);
- its `fov`, the one the camera is named for (120, 70 or 30).

Any other camera is refused (`ValueError`, before anything is generated for it), as is a second CARLA camera for one Hyperion camera, and a `camera_map` naming a camera not in `cameras`. Its mount on the ego is free.

`python -m cosmos_canvas.sim_auto.camera <camera> [--resolution 480]` prints the camera as an [autoware_carla_scenario](https://github.com/autowarefoundation/autoware_carla_scenario) `driver.cameras` entry, with its F-theta `lens` (principal point, both polynomials and which one defines the lens, maximum angle and `linear_cde`, in the generated frames' pixels):

```yaml
- logical_id: "camera_front_wide_120fov"
  image_width: 832
  image_height: 480
  fov: 120.0
  lens:
    model: "ftheta"
    principal_point: [412.641208, 242.790324]
    pixeldist_to_angle_poly: [0.0, 0.00243165382, -4.05625414e-08, 6.10818843e-10, -9.93181484e-13, 8.73787714e-16]
    angle_to_pixeldist_poly: [0.0, 411.286296, 2.40299018, -16.2530848, 10.3586782, -3.39986006]
    max_angle: 1.226783919
    linear_cde: [1.00234926, 0.0, 0.0]
    reference_poly: "pixeldist_to_angle"
```

### Limits

- Traffic-light states are not sent by carla_canvas, so the lights are drawn "unknown" in the controls.
- The rollout's length is set up front (`horizon_seconds`). Past it, the sensors are dark.
- Every configured camera needs a CARLA camera on the ego, mapped by `camera_map` (role name to camera key) or by its yaw, and only the generated cameras are cameras (see [Cameras](#cameras)). The ego needs one ray-cast LiDAR; without one, a virtual LiDAR 1.94 m above the ego is used. Further LiDARs get no returns.
- Generated LiDAR is the Pandar128's: +15° to −25°, 5 to 100 m.
